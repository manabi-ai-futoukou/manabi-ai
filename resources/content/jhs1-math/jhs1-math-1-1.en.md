# Numbers with Signs

## Goals of This Lesson

- You will learn that there are numbers smaller than 0, and be able to describe them with the words "positive number" and "negative number."
- You will be able to use positive and negative numbers to express quantities that have opposite meanings.

## Numbers Smaller Than 0

Have you ever heard a winter weather forecast say, "Tomorrow's lowest temperature will be 3 degrees below freezing"?

If we decide that the temperature at which water freezes is 0 ℃, then a temperature 3 ℃ lower is below 0 ℃. We write this temperature as $-3$ ℃ and read it as "negative 3 degrees" (or "minus 3 degrees").

The numbers you learned in elementary school were 0 and numbers greater than 0. In junior high school, **numbers smaller than 0** are added to these.

- A number greater than 0 is called a **positive number**. A positive number is sometimes written with a **positive sign $+$** (plus), like $+5$. $+5$ and $5$ are the same number.
- A number smaller than 0 is called a **negative number**. A negative number is written with a **negative sign $-$** (minus), like $-3$.
- **0 is neither a positive number nor a negative number.**

Until now, you have used $+$ and $-$ as symbols for "add" and "subtract." Here, we are using them as the **signs** of numbers. Keep in mind that the same symbols have two different roles.

## Sorting Numbers into Groups

Integers can be divided into the following three groups.

| Negative integers | 0 | Positive integers |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| $\cdots,\ -3,\ -2,\ -1$ | $0$ | $1,\ 2,\ 3,\ \cdots$ |

The positive integers $1,\ 2,\ 3,\ \cdots$ are also called **natural numbers**. 0 is not a natural number.

There are also negative decimals and fractions. For example, $-0.5$ and $-\dfrac{2}{3}$ are negative numbers.

## Quantities with Opposite Meanings

If we use negative numbers, we can express **quantities that have opposite meanings to each other** together, using one kind of word.

For example, on a road that runs east and west, let's say that "going 3 km east" is written as $+3$ km. Then the opposite, "going 2 km west," can be written as $-2$ km.

| What we decide to show with a positive number | What is shown with a negative number |
| :-- | :-- |
| Going east | Going west |
| Income | Expense |
| Increase | Decrease |
| Higher than the standard | Lower than the standard |

Which one to make positive is decided by you at the start. What matters is to **make clear the standard (the place that corresponds to 0) and the positive direction**.

We can also decide on a standard and show how much more or less something is compared with it. Suppose your goal for reading each day is 30 minutes. On a day you read for 40 minutes, that is 10 minutes more than the goal, so it is $+10$ minutes. On a day you read for 25 minutes, that is 5 minutes less, so it is $-5$ minutes.

## Worked Example

Express the following quantities using a positive sign or a negative sign.

1. A temperature 4.5 ℃ lower than 0 ℃
2. When "going 6 m north" is written as $+6$ m, "going 8 m south"

**How to Think About It**

1. A temperature lower than 0 ℃ is expressed with a negative number. The answer is $-4.5$ ℃.
2. Going north is expressed with a positive number, so going south, the opposite, is a negative number. The answer is $-8$ m.

## Check Your Understanding

1. From the following numbers, choose all the negative numbers.
   $+7,\quad -2,\quad 0,\quad -0.3,\quad \dfrac{5}{2},\quad -\dfrac{4}{5}$
2. From the following numbers, choose all the natural numbers.
   $-3,\quad 0,\quad 1,\quad 2.5,\quad 8$
3. When "an income of 200 yen" is written as $+200$ yen, how can "an expense of 350 yen" be written?
4. Rewrite "$-5$ kg heavier" in a way that does not use a negative number.

### Answers

1. $-2,\quad -0.3,\quad -\dfrac{4}{5}$
2. $1,\quad 8$
3. $-350$ yen
4. 5 kg lighter
