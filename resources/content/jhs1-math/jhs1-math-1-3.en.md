# Addition

- You will be able to use the commutative law and the associative law of addition to calculate in clever ways.

## Thinking by Moving on a Number Line

Adding is called **addition**, and the result of addition is called the **sum**.

Let's use the number line you learned about in the last lesson to think of addition as "movement." We decide that **adding a positive number means moving to the right, and adding a negative number means moving to the left**. We always start from the origin (0).

First, let's think about $(+2) + (+3)$. From the origin, move 2 to the right, and then move 3 more to the right from there.

```numberline

```

We arrive at $+5$. So $(+2) + (+3) = +5$.

Next, let's think about $(-2) + (-3)$. From the origin, move 2 to the left, and then move 3 more to the left from there.

```numberline
{"min":-7,"max":7,"arrows":[{"from":0,"to":-2,"label":"-2","color":"blue"},{"from":-2,"to":-5,"label":"-3","color":"blue"}],"points":[{"at":-5,"label":"Answer","color":"green"}]}
```

We arrive at $-5$. So $(-2) + (-3) = -5$.

## The Sum of Two Numbers with the Same Sign

In the two examples above, we moved twice in the same direction, so the total distance moved was $2 + 3 = 5$. The direction is the same as that of the original numbers.

> **The sum of two numbers with the same sign**
> Add their absolute values, and put the common sign on the result.

## The Sum of Two Numbers with Different Signs

Let's think about $(+3) + (-5)$. From the origin, move 3 to the right, then change direction and move 5 to the left.

```numberline
{"min":-7,"max":7,"arrows":[{"from":0,"to":3,"label":"+3","color":"red"},{"from":3,"to":-2,"label":"-5","color":"blue"}],"points":[{"at":-2,"label":"Answer","color":"green"}]}
```

We arrive at $-2$. We moved 3 to the right and 5 to the left, so the leftward movement was larger by $5 - 3 = 2$, and we ended up on the left side of the origin.

$$(+3) + (-5) = -(5 - 3) = -2$$

> **The sum of two numbers with different signs**
> Subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger absolute value, and put the sign of the number with the larger absolute value on the result.

The sum of two numbers that have equal absolute values and different signs is 0. This is because if you move 4 to the right and then 4 to the left, you come back to the origin where you started.

$$(+4) + (-4) = 0$$

Also, adding 0 does not change a number. When you add a number to 0, you get that number.

$$(-6) + 0 = -6 \qquad 0 + (-6) = -6$$

## Laws of Calculation for Addition

The following laws, which you learned in elementary school, also hold for addition of positive and negative numbers.

- **Commutative law of addition**　$a + b = b + a$
- **Associative law of addition**　$(a + b) + c = a + (b + c)$

Because of these two laws, when you add several numbers, **you may change the order or the grouping of the numbers when you calculate.** If you first put together the positive numbers and put together the negative numbers, the calculation becomes easier.

## Worked Example

Let's do the following calculation.

$$(+8) + (-5) + (+2) + (-9)$$

**How to Think About It**

First, gather the positive numbers together and the negative numbers together.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(+8) + (-5) + (+2) + (-9) &= (+8) + (+2) + (-5) + (-9) \\
&= (+10) + (-14) \\
&= -(14 - 10) \\
&= -4
\end{aligned}
$$
